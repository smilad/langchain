## Mental model
The evaluator has three deliberately distinct layers. Deterministic retrieval checks verify current authorized metadata and expected-evidence recall. Deterministic output checks verify source membership and whether abstention matches the case label. Semantic support is explicitly not a deterministic check. A row can pass membership and still contain a wrong number, omit a condition, or resolve conflicting policies without evidence. Expected abstention labels are teaching targets, not substitutes for reading a nuanced response.

Human review scores groundedness, relevance and correctness from 0 to 2, records abstention appropriateness, and maps substantive claims to quoted evidence. A reviewer must consider source revision, access boundary, units and conditions, not just stylistic confidence. Supply reviewer identity and rationale; do not auto-fill a human judgment from the model's grade. The README describes the optional JSON review format. Unreviewed fields remain null rather than invented passes.

--judge adds a real paid structured-output model grader. Its grade, rationale, usage and elapsed time appear separately from generation and human review. The grader sees the question, rubric, actual answer and supplied evidence. The instructions require claim-to-evidence mapping, but those instructions do not make it an oracle. The same provider may share biases with the answer model, and malicious documents may also target the evaluator. No grader feeds an automated correction loop.

Calibrate on development examples: independently rate actual outputs, compare dimensions and record disagreement. A false positive on the human-authored 900-day example should trigger investigation of the judge, not changes to the human rubric. Inspect multiple runs when grading variability matters. For held-out reporting keep the frozen configuration and treat both grader and human disagreement as review evidence, not an excuse to tune until a favorable score appears.

## Data/control flow
`Actual result/evidence → deterministic checks; independently → human rubric; optionally → fallible paid model opinion`
